* Galaxies other than the MW

#+CAPTION: /JWST/ deep field showing a field of galaxies
[[./images/JWST_HST_deepfield.webp]]

A decade ago, the Hubble space telescope (named after the person who
measured the distance to M31 using Leavitt's law for Cepheids) was
pointed for a very long time (11.3 days of continuous exposure) to
/nothing/, a dark area of the sky with no known sources. This crazy
observational program revealed faint and far galaxies, creating the
"/HST/ deep field" images. Since then, this experiment has been
repeated, increasing exposures and thus depth of the observations, and
re-done with /JWST/, which was in part designed with the scientific goal
of studying galaxies in the early Universe.

*N.B.:* /JWST/ has a bigger field of view, and more sensitive IR cameras
with a bigger mirror, so it can outcompete /HST/ optical and UV sensors
in a shorter exposure time.

** Galaxy density

Based on observations like these (but typically performed on a wider
area of the sky, with a trade-off of going less deep), we can estimate
the /number density of galaxies/ in the Universe.

However, we already know that not all galaxies have the same mass (the
MW is much larger than both LMC /and/ SM for example!), so we may want
to look not just at the number density, but the number density as a
function of galaxy mass.

Because the mass-budget is /dominated/ by DM, which we cannot directly
/see/, we can use the /stellar mass/ $M_{\star}$ as a proxy, knowing that for
example for the MW $M_{\rm tot} \simeq M_{\star} \times 1000$. In the figure below, from
[[https://www.aanda.org/articles/aa/full_html/2025/03/aa52570-24/aa52570-24.html][Shuntov et al. 2025]], each color the number of galaxies per (10^{6}pc)^{3}
elements of volume and as a function of their $M_{\star}$ on the x-axis for
one spherical shell of the Universe centered around us:

#+CAPTION: /JWST/ galaxy mass function for different slices of the Universe (each color correspond to a certain spherical shell of the Universe, labeled by the redshift $z$). Note the log-log scale.
#+ATTR_HTML: :width 50%
[[file:images/gal_density.png]]

Black curves are closer to us: anywhere in the Universe, small
galaxies outnumber large galaxies (which you would expect in the
inside out repeated mergers picture of galaxy evolution) -- the curves
slope downwards!

Moreover, earlier in the Universe (lighter colors corresponding to
farther distances and thus galaxies seen earlier in the Universe's
evolution) there are fewer galaxies.

Since its launch, /JWST/ has been revealing very large galaxies very
early in the Universe, possibly challenging key elements of the
paradigm for galaxy evolution and cosmology.

*N.B.:* We can use the number of galaxies per unit volume in a given
mass-bin to convert estimates of events /per galaxy/ into an intrinsic
rate density per unit time:

#+begin_latex
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal{R} = \frac{\# \ events}{Galaxy}\times \frac{\# Galaxies}{Volume} \ \ .
\end{equation}
#+end_latex


* Galaxy zoo
Galaxies exhibit a diverse set of morphological properties, in terms
of their shape, size, and colors, as you can see from the collection
of NASA images classified by galaxy type [[https://science.nasa.gov/universe/galaxies/types/#intro][here]].

The [[https://zoo4.galaxyzoo.org/][Galaxy zoo]] project is an ongoing citizen-scientist effort to crowd
source the visual classification of galaxies: this was used to label
training set for machine learning classifications, but also provided
unexpected discoveries, such as green pea galaxies.

#+CAPTION: "Green pea" galaxies are spherical in shape, green in color, and usually very isolated: the current thinking is that these may be neaby analogs of the galaxies that caused the reionizatin of the Universe.
[[./images/GP.jpg]]

This shows two things:
- we have so many observational data that professional astronomers
  /need/ the help of amateurs to parse through them (which is useful to
  design algorithms to then have computers do what thousands of
  amateurs can collectively do, but faster)
- there is so much unknown out there that dedicated amateur
  astronomers and citizen scientists can contribute crucial insights
  and even find and name entire new /classes/ of astronomical objects,
  green pea galaxies being just one example.

** Spiral

We have already encountered spiral galaxies, the MW is /one/ of them.
These are big galaxies, with disks containing most ongoing star
formation, spiral arms that compress the gas (driving the stars
together until they can trigger the formation of new stars from the
gas), an extended halo composed of highly inclined and eccentric
orbits of mostly old stars dynamically perturbed.

#+CAPTION: Composite image of the "[[https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spiral_arm#/media/File:Messier51_sRGB.jpg][whirpool galaxy]]" or Messier 51 (M51) another example of spiral galaxy, seen face on (galaxies are randomly oriented in the sky).
#+ATTR_HTML: :width 50%
[[./images/whirpool.jpg]]

NASA maintains [[https://science.nasa.gov/category/universe/galaxies/spiral-galaxies/][here]] a catalog of composite images of spiral galaxies,
which shows the diversity of features that can occur in a spiral
galaxy. Recall that galaxies are /never/ in equilibrium because of
flybys and mergers within a galaxy cluster! Therefore, by sampling the
diversity of (spiral) galaxies in the Universe we understand which
morphologies are easily realized and occur often in nature (ergodicity
= studying distributions across space to understand distribution in
time).

Spiral galaxies usually are blue and rich in new stars forming in
their disks. They are also the typical hosts of core-collapse SNe (the
death of short-lived massive stars).

** Elliptical

Elliptical galaxies appear red, so their are rich in old, evolved, low
mass stars, and don't have much active star formation. They are
usually more massive and sit deeper in the DM halo of a galaxy cluster.

#+CAPTION: NGC2865 is an example of elliptical galaxy.
[[./images/NGC2865.jpeg]]

Their "round" shape is because all the orbits in a hypothetical
initial disk are perturbed onto high inclination, high eccentricity
orbits, their larger mass and older age means they had more time for
galaxy mergers to generate perturbations, and many stellar orbits.

They can host SNIa (WD explosions), but rarely (if at all) host
transient astrophysical sources related to massive stars.

** Lenticular
This is an intermediate class between spiral and elliptical: they are
disk-y galaxies, but with oversized bulges and no spiral arms. They
tend to be dust rich and may be a transitional class.

#+CAPTION: Example of lenticular galaxy NGC4886.
[[./images/NGC4886.jpeg]]

** Irregular
Many (most) small galaxies have irregular shapes: they are never given
the time to settle on equilibrium because of the interaction with
other galaxies.

We have already seen irregular dwarf galaxies, for example the LMC/SMC
pair.
