# Top hat questions

* Recap and aims of this lecture

We have seen that [[https://science.nasa.gov/universe/galaxies/types/][galaxies come in many shapes]], they are typically out
of dynamical equilibrium because of the constant perturbations by
nearby galaxies in their host galaxy cluster.

Typically the vast majority of their mass is in the form of "dark
matter", which forms a (as far as we know) /spherical/ halo much bigger
than the galaxy itself.

We have seen that in the center of the MW (the galaxy that hosts the
solar system), there is a "supermassive BH" (SMBH) called Sag A^{*}, with
mass $M\simeq4\times10^{6}\,M_{\odot}$.

#+CAPTION: EHT radio-interferometry image of Sag A^{*}.
#+ATTR_HTML: :width 50%
[[./images/EHT_sagastar.jpg]]

This was first /inferred/ from the Keplerian motion of stars in the
innermost part of the galaxy, which constrain the total mass and the
maximum extent of this mass distribution. Later, the tenuous accretion
disk around it was resolved through radio-interferometry by EHT.

Sag A^{*} was actually the /second/ SMBH imaged by EHT, after they released
this image of the accretion disk and the shade of the BH in the galaxy
M87 (one of the 110 objects in the Messier catalog!):

#+CAPTION: Right: X-ray image of the center of the galaxy M87. Left: zoom-in with the EHT radio-interferometry.
[[./images/M87EHT.webp]]

These two SMBH and host galaxies (MW and M87) can both be imaged by
EHT: the resolution it can achieve is of \sim25 \mu arcsecond (recall [[file:notes-lecture-02-scale.org::*Angular distances and size][1
arcsec = 1/60 arcminutes and 1 arcminute = 1/60 degrees]]). In the MW,
Sag A^{*} has a mass of $4\times10^{6}\,M_{\odot}$ and distance of $D\sim 8\,\mathrm{kpc}$,
while M87 has a much more massive BH of $6\times10^{9}\,M_{\odot}$ and is at a
distance of $D\sim16\,000\,\mathrm{kpc}$.

The ratio $M_{\rm SMBH}/D\sim 0.5\times10^{3} M_{\odot} \mathrm{pc}^{-1}$ is the same for both
these objects! We will see below that the radius of a BH is
proportional to it's mass, so $M_{\rm SMBH}/D\propto R_{\rm SMBH}/D$: Sag A^{*} and
M87 SMBH have the same angular size on the sky, making them both
viable targets for EHT.

* What is a black hole?
Until the \sim late 1960s, BHs were thought to be a "mathematical
curiosity", a particular solution of Einstein's equations that
describe gravity beyond the Newtonian model, with general relativity
(GR).

** The core idea of general relativity
We cannot derive here GR, but we can focus on the key point that
conceptually updates Newton's law of gravity. In Newtonian gravity
/space-time/ is a fixed structure in which gravity can propagate at
infinite speed: if you remove the Sun, immediately the Earth lacks the
centripetal gravitational pull to stay on its orbit and is launched
along the tangent.

Einstein (in his "annus mirabilis" 1905) showed however that he could
derive all laws of electromagnetism in vacuum /postulating/ that the
speed of light in vacuum is the maximum speed for the propagation of
information (e.g., the info that the Sun is still there pulling the
Earth onto its orbit): this means Newtonian gravity is fundamentally
incompatible with /special relativity/, the theory that correctly
describes the dynamics of objects approaching the speed of light.

In 1916, Einstein (and shortly after Hilbert -- no lone geniuses, but
an active and debating community!) published his theory of /general
relativity/ that fixes this problem.

In this picture, gravity is actually an "apparent force" due to the
/curvature of space-time/ (much like the centrifugal force in a rotating
frame arising because the frame is not inertial): the Earth orbit's
the Sun not because of it's attraction working as a centripetal force,
but because the Sun's mass curves space time around it and the Earth
is trying to "go straight on a curved surface", like the interior of a
bowl!

#+CAPTION: Space-time represented as a 2D plane (green grid) deformed by the massive object (the Sun): the deformed "shape" of space-time causes straight lines to "bend" acting like if there were a centripetal force.
[[./images/space-time-curv.jpg]]

Like any good /new/ theory of something well understood empirically, GR
reduces for small masses and low velocities to Newtonian gravity, and
/extends/ it to a new framework for large masses and speeds close to the
speed of light. Einstein's field equation describes /how masses bend
space-time/ and at the same time /how space-time imposes to free-falling
masses to move/ (i.e., how masses only under the influence of gravity
move):

#+begin_latex
\begin{equation}\label{eq:einstein_eq}
 \mathrm{Space-time\ curvature} \Leftrightarrow \mathrm{Mass\ distribution} \ \ .
\end{equation}
#+end_latex

** Black holes
One week only after Einstein's publication, Karl Schwarzschild, from a
hospital bed during WWI, published solutions to Einstein's equation
for a spherically symmetric space time around a point mass.

*N.B.:* The assumption of spherical symmetry helps in the calculations!
Schwarzschild looked for the simplest solution possible first!

What he found is that there is a region of space around a mass with
radius $r_{g} = \frac{GM}{c^{2}}$ such that it would take infinite energy
for an object within this region to escape the gravity of the mass
$M$. In other word, the /escape velocity/ from within this region is
faster than $c$ and no object/information can reach it! This means not
even photons can escape this region, and thus the name "black hole".

*N.B.:* far away from this, $r\gg r_{g}$ the space-time of Schwarzschild's
solution becomes the "flat" spacetime of Newtonian gravity as
expected.

#+CAPTION: Infographic on a BHs.
[[./images/BHinfographic.jpg]]


So technically, /inside/ a BH matter (but $E=m\gamma c^{2}$, so all energy)
falls towards the center, where "space-time" is "punctured" in GR.
This singularity has $r\rightarrow0$ and $\rho\rightarrow+\infty$: the appearance of an infinite
density suggests that interactions other than gravity will start to
play a role: to treat the physics at the singularity we need a model
of /gravity/ (described by GR) *and* the other forces (described by
quantum mechanics, QM). These two are still separate field without a
synthesis between them: ultimately this suggests that $\rho\rightarrow+\infty$ is a
symptom that we cannot ignore QM anymore but we don't have a better
theory to use.

At the even of horizon though (the surface that has escape velocity
$c$) nothing physically special is happening: if you were passing
through the event of horizon, *you* wouldn't notice anything special (a
part from the fact that the gas around you is getting very hot, and
gravity is stretching you, these things were happening at $r_{g}+\varepsilon$ and
will continue at $r_{g}-\varepsilon$ for small distances $\varepsilon$).




*N.B.:* from 1916 (Einstein's first paper and Schwarzschild solution
of the equations), it took \sim40 years for Roy Kerr to find a second
solution, for a spacetime around a "rotating" point mass in 1963.

** Orbital motion of stars

** Gravitational-waves
*** merger of two stellar-mass BHs
*** Future: LISA pulsar timing arrays for SMBH
