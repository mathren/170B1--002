Study guide for 1st midterm on 2026-09-30 Wed

The midterm will be administered in our usual classroom (N210) via TopHat and is closed book. You will be allowed one 3” x 5” notecard (double sided).

Neev will hold a review session in the Parker Library (Steward N304) on 2026-09-24 Thu from 5-6:30pm.

This outline is organized according to the lectures (see also schedule). However, you are responsible for more material than is represented by the lectures. In particular, study:

  • Lecture notes
  • Weekly homework assignments: Labs 1 & 2 and your Mastering Astronomy Assignments 1, 3, 4 and Telescopes Tutorial. The Study Area is very useful for practice!
  • Textbook: The Cosmic Perspective, Chapters 1-6 (eText access via D2L)

Key concepts

Week 1

Reading: Chapters 1, 2

Questions/concepts:

  • Relative sizes of the Universe/Galaxy/Solar System/Earth
  • What is a light-year?
  • How can we see into the past?
  • Ages and relative ages of the Universe/Earth/humanity
  • Celestial motions:
    • What is the ecliptic?
    • Why do the stars go around the sky?
    • Why can you never see some stars from Tucson?
  • Seasons:
    • What causes the seasons on Earth?
    • What effects change the amount of sunlight received over the year?
    • How does the Earth-Sun distance change over the year?
  • Lunar phases:
    • What determines the phase of the moon?
    • Relate positions of Moon/Earth/Sun in each phase
  • Eclipses:
    • What is a Lunar/Solar eclipse?
    • When do they happen? How often?
    • What moon phases are involved?

Week 2 Reading: Chapter 3 Lecture Slides: Lectures 3, 4 (Lab 1) Questions/concepts: Planetary Motion What is retrograde motion? Which planets can move this way? Why was it hard to explain this in early models of the “universe”? What is the geocentric model of the universe? How did it explain retrograde motion? What were Greek contributions to the development of science? What are the hallmarks of science? Copernicus/Tycho/Kepler/Galileo Who were they? Roughly when did they live? What were their main contributions? What evidence did Galileo provide for the heliocentric model? Kepler’s Laws of planetary motion: What are they? Make sure you can apply them to our Solar System How is the process of science illustrated in the story of our model of the Solar System? Motion/Momentum/Angular Momentum/Force/Weight What do each of these concepts mean? How do force/acceleration/velocity relate?

Week 3 Reading: Chapter 4 Lecture Slides: Lectures 5, 6 Questions/concepts: Newton Who was he? Roughly when did he live? What were his major contributions? How is the moon’s orbit like an apple falling? Newton’s Laws of Motion What are Newton’s three Laws of Motion? How do they explain forces between objects and the relative accelerations of objects of different masses? How does gravitational force depend on mass and distance? What is the origin of the tides? What force and what bodies are responsible? Conservation Laws: What is a conservation law? What is angular momentum? How does it relate to orbits? What are astronomically relevant forms of energy? What are examples of conversion between those forms of energy? Weeks 4 & 5 Reading: Chapters 5, 6 Lecture Slides: Lectures 7, 8, 9 (Lab 2) Questions/concepts: Wave/Particle Light How is light a wave? How are frequency and wavelength related? How is light a particle (photon)? How are frequency and photon energy related? What are types of light in the electromagnetic spectrum? Which are the most/least energetic? How do light and matter interact? Why is the Sun yellow? Why is a tree green? Atoms and Energy Levels Why do atoms emit/absorb in spectral lines? What changes in an atom when a photon is absorbed/emitted? What types of spectra are there? How is each made? Continuous spectra What is a blackbody? How does the blackbody spectral peak depend on temperature? How does blackbody luminosity depend on temperature? Learning from spectra How do spectra tell us what elements are present? How do we measure velocity from spectral lines? How do we measure temperature from spectra? Telescopes Why do we need telescopes? What determines where we put them? What are the important properties and advantages of telescopes?
